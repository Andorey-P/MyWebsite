# CTA Copy Patterns

Reusable calls-to-action for creative-developer portfolios, with the formula behind each and real good/bad pairs. Full strategic context in [../docs/narrative-structure.md](../docs/narrative-structure.md) (CTA is the conversion step of the arc) and [../docs/recruiter-employer-psychology.md](../docs/recruiter-employer-psychology.md) (why gated/vague CTAs lose recruiters specifically).

## The formula

A strong portfolio CTA has three parts: **what happens next** (not just "contact me" — contact for *what*), **a low-friction path** (a real email/link, not just a form), and **light framing that restates the thesis or implies demand** (selectivity, availability, specificity about the kind of work wanted).

```
[What happens if you click] + [how, concretely] + [optional: framing/selectivity]
```

## Hero CTA (primary — points at work, not contact)

Formula: an imperative pointed at proof.

- Good: `View Work ↓` / `See the Work` / `Selected Work →`
- Bad: `Learn More` (learn more about what — no object, no reason to click)
- Bad: `Get in Touch` as the *primary, loudest* hero CTA — asks for commitment before any evidence has been shown. Fine as the visually secondary option; wrong as the leading one. See [../docs/hero-section-strategy.md](../docs/hero-section-strategy.md).

## Site-wide / footer CTA (the main conversion ask)

Formula: name what the visitor should do, name the kind of work you want, give a real path.

- Good:
  > **Have a brief that needs real-time 3D done properly?**
  > I take on a small number of freelance projects each quarter. Tell me about it — [name@domain.dev](#) or [book 20 minutes](#).
- Good (in-house/full-time targeting instead of freelance):
  > **Looking for a creative developer who ships, not just prototypes?**
  > Open to select full-time roles — reach out at [name@domain.dev](#) or [LinkedIn](#).
- Bad: `Contact Me` with no further context — no object, no path preference, no framing. Passes the reader zero information about what happens if they click.
- Bad: a required multi-field form (name, company, budget range, "how did you hear about us") as the *only* contact path. Every additional required field is friction a recruiter — often reaching out to several candidates in one sitting — will route around. Keep the form optional, or keep it to name + email + message; always pair it with a visible direct email/LinkedIn.

## Case-study-end CTA (soft, contextual — not a repeat of the site-wide ask)

Formula: reference the specific project just read, invite a related conversation.

- Good:
  > Working on something that needs this kind of real-time work? [Let's talk](#).
- Bad: repeating the exact site-wide CTA verbatim after every project — reads as templated and adds nothing the reader hasn't already seen.
- Also acceptable: no CTA at all at the end of individual case studies, relying on the single site-wide CTA — valid if the portfolio is a single continuous scroll rather than separate project pages. Don't stack a strong CTA at the end of every section *and* a final one; pick one point of maximum emphasis.

## Availability/status framing (used in hero or about, functions as a soft CTA)

Formula: concrete status + implied selectivity, never fake urgency.

- Good: `Available for select freelance projects` / `Open to new roles — actively interviewing` / `Currently booked through [month] — taking inquiries for [next quarter]`
- Bad: `Hurry, limited availability!` — fake urgency reads as sales copy, which undercuts the craft-led tone the rest of a strong portfolio establishes.
- Bad: leaving availability unstated entirely when it's actually a selling point (busy = in demand) — silence here is a missed trust signal, not neutral.

## When NOT to use a hard CTA at all

If the portfolio's positioning is exploratory/art-first rather than hire-me (see the exception noted in [../docs/hero-section-strategy.md](../docs/hero-section-strategy.md)), a softer closing note ("Curious what I'm building next? [Follow along](#)") can outperform a direct "hire me" CTA that would clash with the site's tone. Default to the direct formulas above unless that positioning has been explicitly chosen.
