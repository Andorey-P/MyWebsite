# Design Critique Output Template

Use for "does this feel cohesive/premium/right" requests, working from [checklists/critique-checklist.md](../checklists/critique-checklist.md) for a single page/section or [checklists/consistency-audit-checklist.md](../checklists/consistency-audit-checklist.md) for a cross-page audit. Fill in every section — do not skip to the verdict without the evidence sections, and do not soften the verdict into a hedge.

---

## Scope

What was reviewed (one page, one section, the whole site) and against what stated point of view, if one exists. If no point of view was stated going in, say so — this itself is often the root finding.

## Verdict

One direct sentence: does this cohere as one artistic vision, yes or no, or partially with the specific condition stated (e.g. "yes on the homepage and case-study pages; no on the about page"). No hedging language ("it depends," "sort of") without immediately naming the condition that would flip the answer.

## What's working (be specific)

For each genuinely strong element, name it precisely and say *why* it works — tie it to a specific principle from this Skill's docs, not a vague compliment.

- **[Element]** — [why it works, one to two sentences, referencing the specific mechanism: focal contrast, mood-translation consistency, pairing logic, etc.]

## What's breaking coherence

Ranked by leverage — the fix that would most improve the felt experience first, not in the order issues were noticed.

### 1. [Highest-leverage issue]

- **Where:** specific page/section/element
- **What:** the specific visual fact (a color, a typeface, a spacing/density choice, a motion register) — not "feels off"
- **Why it breaks coherence:** which principle it violates and how (references [docs/](../docs/) as appropriate)
- **Fix:** the specific, concrete correction — a hex value, a typeface swap, a named composition adjustment, a motion register correction. Not "make it more consistent."

### 2. [Next issue]

(Same structure. Include as many as are real findings — don't pad to a fixed count, and don't stop short if there are more real issues.)

## What NOT to touch

Explicitly name anything a less experienced pass might "fix" that would actually hurt the piece — e.g. "the asymmetric hero placement looks unbalanced at first glance but is intentional tension per the aggressive mood brief; don't center it." This prevents a well-meaning follow-up pass from undoing a deliberate choice.

## Prioritized action list

Top 3–5 items, highest impact first, each one sentence, each actionable today.

## Handoff notes

Anything surfaced during this critique that belongs to a sibling skill — state it in one sentence per item, don't solve it here.

- `ui-ux-designer`: —
- `portfolio-storytelling`: —
- `animation-principles`: —
